Tibor Miklós is a British-Hungarian sound and visual artist on the Isle of Man. His work starts with listening to a place for long enough that it starts talking back.

Most of what he makes now is one of two things.

The first is music written for a particular place. A chapel, a church, a stretch of coast: the building or the landscape becomes the instrument. He records what is already there, the harmonium, the pews, the wind in the churchyard, and captures how the room answers a sound, then builds the piece from that and nothing else. The *Organism* series started in the Methodist chapel at Agneash and carried on at St Maughold's Church with *Layers*.

The second is the listening experience. The finished piece goes back to the place it came from and plays there, so people can sit in the building and hear it in the room it was written for. Usually there is no start and no end. People come in, stay as long as they like, and go.

He keeps the tools simple: a field recorder, a modular synth, a film camera, pen and ink. Sound comes before music. A colour, a timbre, the smallest hint of a rhythm is often the way into a piece. Ideas are left alone until they are ready, and what comes out is usually simpler than what went in, and more alive.

As a multidisciplinary audio and visual artist he is interested in how technology is shaping the future of creativity and art. He runs Universal Magic, an ongoing experimental audiovisual practice made with other artists, which responds to and challenges the way we create and consume culture and art in an age of rapid technological change.

He also plays live: film scores with Kitterland, improvised electronics with Busy Lady, and solo sets on modular.

He carries a European outlook into a practice rooted on a small island in the Irish Sea.
